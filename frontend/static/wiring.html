02 / Build the small circuits correctly

Enlarged views · exact endpoints · module variants explained

01 / Power distribution

Three logic rails. A separate pump supply.

Unplug USB and pump power while attaching wires.

  • Use three distinct breadboard strips for 3.3 V, 5 V and GND. Check for breaks halfway along long power rails.
  • Use the board's 5 V/VIN pin as a USB-derived supply only if its pinout confirms that connection. Do not feed external 5 V into that pin while USB is attached.
  • If USB cannot supply the relay coil and sensors reliably, power those accessories from a separate regulated 5 V supply; join its negative to logic GND, not its positive to USB/VIN.
  • Keep the pump power loop separate. Its negative goes only to the pump and flyback diode, not to the ESP32 ground rail.
02 / Capacitive soil moisture v1.2

Use the analog output on GPIO32.

GPIO32 is ADC1, so it works while Wi-Fi is active.

Power this sensor from 3.3 V so its analog signal stays in the ESP32 input range. Only insert the coated probe section into soil; keep the connector and electronics above the soil line.

After connecting, record dry and wet ADC values for SOIL_DRY_ADC and SOIL_WET_ADC. The current defaults are placeholders. An open analog pin can float; a plausible value alone does not prove a good connection.

03 / DHT22 · AM2302

DATA needs a pull-up to 3.3 V.

Bare four-pin DHT22: grille facing you, legs pointing down.

The 4.7 kΩ resistor connects between DATA and 3.3 V, not in series with the signal. Leave pin 3 unconnected.

Three-pin module or cabled AM2302? Use its VCC / +, DATA / OUT and GND / − labels; the physical order and wire colors can differ. Omit the extra resistor only if the module already has a pull-up to its 3.3 V supply.

Keep VCC and the data pull-up at 3.3 V in this build. A pull-up to 5 V must not connect directly to GPIO33.

04 / YF-S201 · 5 V pulse-output reference

The yellow wire goes through a divider.

Use 10 kΩ above the tap and 15 kΩ below it, preferably 1% resistors.

The junction between the two resistors is the only signal connection to GPIO25. Flow sensor ground, divider ground and ESP32 ground are joined. The top and bottom resistors are not interchangeable.

This 10 kΩ / 15 kΩ pair gives more voltage margin than the older 10 kΩ / 20 kΩ example. It changes no firmware settings. Verify the divider high level is within 3.3 V before connecting GPIO25.

If your sensor has an open-collector output

Check its actual datasheet/output circuit. A verified open-collector output without an internal 5 V pull-up can instead use a 4.7–10 kΩ pull-up from yellow to 3.3 V and connect yellow to GPIO25, with the divider removed. Do not assume every YF-S201 clone has that output type.

A typical YF-S201 is specified around 1–30 L/min. Your tiny pump may not produce enough flow to turn its rotor. Zero pulses can occur even with water moving; calibrate using a measured volume and check the sensor's actual datasheet. The firmware raises flow_fault when the pump has run for 10 s without a pulse, so a disconnected sensor cannot look like a valid 0 L/min reading.
05 / Relay input side

5 V coil does not mean 3.3 V input.

The uploaded firmware expects an active-LOW relay on GPIO27.

Confirm the relay module before attaching W22. Direct GPIO27 → IN is valid only for a module explicitly compatible with 3.3 V control, with no 5 V pull-up reaching the GPIO. The exact input circuitry of your module has not been identified.

The drawing assumes a VCC / GND / IN module. For boards with JD-VCC, jumpers or selectable trigger polarity, follow that module's schematic. Do not infer terminal order from the drawing.

Active-LOW 5 V input only? Use a transistor interface

An NPN open-collector stage avoids putting the relay's 5 V input on the ESP32. It inverts the control logic: change RELAY_ACTIVE_LOW to false, rebuild and upload before using this alternative.

The 100 kΩ base-to-emitter resistor keeps the transistor OFF during reset. Check the relay's input current against the transistor/interface rating. This alternative is not the direct-input wiring used in the main map.

06 / Relay contacts + mini submersible pump

Switch the pump’s positive lead.

Separate regulated 5 V DC supply, within your pump's 3–6 V rating.

Supply + → COM. NO → pump +. Pump − → supply −. NC is empty. Choose terminal names from the relay's silkscreen; left-to-right order varies between modules.

Fit the diode directly across the motor leads: its silver band points toward pump positive. A reversed diode shorts the supply when the relay closes. Use a diode rated for the motor current; check your pump's startup/stall current against the diode, adapter, wiring and relay's DC contact ratings.

Your pump is 3–6 V: never use the older 12 V BOM example. Do not power the motor from an ESP32 GPIO, 3V3 pin, or the laptop USB rail. Keep the pump submerged during a pump test.

Passives to have ready

1 × 10 kΩ + 1 × 15 kΩ for the flow divider; 1 × 4.7 kΩ for DHT DATA if absent on the module; a correctly rated flyback diode (1N4007 reference shown).

Power & physical assembly

USB data cable, separate regulated 5 V pump supply rated for startup current, breadboard, jumpers, suitable pump leads, tubing and a reservoir. Keep exposed electronics dry.

Optional GPIO13 status LED

GPIO13 → 220–330 Ω resistor → LED anode (long leg). LED cathode (short leg / flat edge) → logic GND. The firmware does not require this LED; an onboard LED may use another GPIO.

04 / First power-up

Verify sensors before switching water.

  1. Keep pump supply unplugged. Inspect polarity, GPIO labels and relay input compatibility. Then connect ESP32 USB.
  2. Measure the 3.3 V and 5 V rails, and check the flow-divider high voltage before attaching its tap to GPIO25.
  3. Open the dashboard and select ESP32-REAL-001. Telemetry should arrive roughly every 15 seconds.
  4. Check DHT readings and dry/wet soil ADC changes. Calibrate soil moisture and flow from measurements.
  5. Verify the relay releases on boot/STOP with pump power still removed. Then connect the pump supply and test briefly with water present.

Look for real telemetry.

device_code: ESP32-REAL-001
simulated: false
temperature_c / humidity_pct: sensor readings
raw_adc / soil_moisture_pct: soil readings
flow_pulse_count / flow_rate_lpm: measured pulses
pump_state: off at boot

These are expected fields, not live readings. null means a missing or invalid reading. Zero flow by itself does not verify the flow sensor. This wiring guide never sends pump commands.

The flashed firmware reads sensors and executes app commands. The earlier moisture/rain auto-stop was simulator-only; this page does not add that control policy to the physical ESP32.
